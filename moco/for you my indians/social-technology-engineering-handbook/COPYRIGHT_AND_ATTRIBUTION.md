## Is it truly open source?

Not in the formal Open Source Initiative sense. Open-source licenses must allow uses such as commercial use and distribution of modified versions. CC BY-NC-ND intentionally restricts those activities. This repository is therefore best described as a **public educational repository** or **source-available learning resource**, not an OSI-approved open-source project.

This distinction is deliberate: it allows people to read and share unchanged copies for non-commercial study while limiting commercial reuse and public distribution of modified versions. If the project later aims to accept community edits and allow broader reuse, the license must be reconsidered before inviting contributions.

## Does this stop every possible copy?

No license can technically prevent someone from copying files. A license sets legal permissions and conditions; it is not DRM and cannot guarantee that infringement will never happen. GitHub's terms also allow users to view and fork public repositories through the platform. A public repository is therefore not a way to make copying physically impossible.

The practical protection is to keep creator credit and license notices visible, publish the repository under the selected terms, retain the release archive and hashes, and document suspected misuse. Do not make threats or claims of guaranteed enforcement; remedies depend on the facts and applicable law.
