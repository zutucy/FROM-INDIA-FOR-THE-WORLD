# 14C — Growth, marketing, network effects, pricing and monetization

## 1. Growth is a system

Growth combines product value, distribution, onboarding, retention, trust and sustainable economics. Buying traffic cannot fix a product that people do not need or trust. A product with strong retention but no reachable audience still needs distribution.

## 2. Distribution channels

Potential channels include search, creator partnerships, communities, app stores, referrals, direct sales, integrations, events, content marketing, paid advertising and partnerships. Evaluate audience fit, cost, conversion, retention and trust—not impressions alone.

Avoid spam, fake engagement, deceptive referrals and scraping contact lists. These tactics harm trust and can breach platform rules or laws.

## 3. Network effects

A network effect occurs when value to a participant changes as more participants join. Direct effect: a messaging tool may be more useful when a user's contacts are present. Indirect effect: more creators can attract viewers, and more viewers can attract creators.

Network effects are not guaranteed. More users can also mean spam, low-quality content, congestion and moderation cost. Measure whether new participants create value for a defined group.

## 4. Cold start problem

A social or marketplace product needs sufficient activity on multiple sides. Possible strategies: begin with one focused community; invite a small group of creators and improve their workflow; seed public content only with permission; support lawful imports/integrations; provide a useful single-player experience before network density exists; and build a repeatable onboarding process.

Do not fabricate users, reviews or activity. Trust is a product asset.

## 5. Funnel and retention

A simple funnel is awareness → visit → signup → activation → first value → repeat use → referral/payment. Measure drop-off and distinguish user choice from technical failure. Retention cohorts show whether users return. Many signups followed by fast abandonment can indicate a mismatch between the promise and first experience.

## 6. Monetization models

- **Subscription:** recurring payment for continuing value.
- **Freemium:** free core with paid capabilities.
- **Usage-based:** price by consumption or transaction.
- **Advertising:** monetize attention/targeting; adds privacy and incentive risks.
- **Marketplace fee:** commission on genuine transactions.
- **Creator tools:** charge for analytics, workflow, storage or business capabilities.
- **Enterprise licence:** sell administration, security, support and integrations.

Select a model aligned with user value and trust. Ads can incentivize more profiling and engagement maximization. Subscriptions require users to see enough value to pay. Marketplace fees require dispute and payment operations.

## 7. Unit economics

Track revenue per paying customer, gross margin after hosting/payment/delivery, customer acquisition cost (CAC), contribution margin, churn/retention, lifetime value assumptions, payback period, moderation/support cost, refunds and fraud.

LTV is an estimate, not a guaranteed future amount. Early-stage assumptions are uncertain. Compare LTV and CAC using consistent cohorts and gross-margin assumptions. Include bandwidth, storage, payment fees, creator payouts, fraud, support and moderation—not just compute.

## 8. Pricing research

Test willingness to pay through transparent pilot offers, paid trials or real purchase behaviour where lawful. Survey statements are weaker than actual behaviour. Explain price, renewal, cancellation, limits and taxes clearly. Do not use dark patterns to make cancellation harder or to trick people into add-ons.

## 9. Advertising and privacy

For ads, document what data supports targeting, which vendors receive it, whether profiling/sensitive categories are involved and what user controls apply. Children and sensitive contexts require special review. Keep measurement separated from private message content and other highly sensitive data unless a lawful and necessary design has been approved.

## 10. Go-to-market experiment

Choose one segment and one channel. Set a small acquisition budget or outreach limit, define activation and 30-day retention, record support/moderation cost and compare cohorts. Stop or change the channel when cost rises without meaningful retained users.

## 11. Exercise

Model 1,000 active users, 5% paid conversion, a monthly price, payment fees, infrastructure, support and creator payouts. Change assumptions and find where margins become negative. List what evidence is needed before believing the model.
